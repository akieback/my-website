About

Speech AI for mental health

I work where speech foundation models meet mental health care. Psychiatric assessment still depends on subjective rating scales, so my research builds speech AI that is interpretable, compute-efficient and auditable: models that show clinicians which parts of a person's speech were flagged, not just a score.

I co-developed S-JEPA, a self-supervised speech model, with collaborators including Yann LeCun and Ravid Shwartz-Ziv at NYU, and applied it in a study of pediatric bipolar disorder with Baylor College of Medicine and Texas Children's Hospital. Related work covers speech-based depression detection, wearable sensing for lung cancer screening, and reliable agentic LLM systems.

Today I am an AI Engineer and Researcher at Discern Science International, where I build the company's mental health research line and work on its structured interview analytics platform. Before that I was a Machine Learning Researcher at the James Silberrad Brown Center for AI at San Diego State University (2023 to 2026).

Research

What I work on

Four lines of work, from foundation models to clinical studies.

Objective speech markers of mental health

A short structured voice interview, encoded with S-JEPA at 20 ms resolution and grouped into 16 interpretable "speech types" without diagnostic labels, tracks how manic a young person is.

r = 0.42 between one speech cluster and clinician-rated mania severity in pediatric bipolar disorder (pilot, N = 30)

Earlier, my depression detection models beat the previous best speech-only result on DAIC-WOZ (macro F1 0.702 vs 0.694) with about 1% of the parameters, and reached 0.72 with a 1.1M-parameter Transformer.

With Baylor College of Medicine and Texas Children's Hospital. Funded by the Milken Institute.

Speech foundation models

S-JEPA replaces the hard k-means targets of HuBERT-style models with soft Gaussian mixture posteriors updated online. It keeps the acoustic ambiguity at phoneme boundaries and removes the offline re-clustering step.

12.10% WER lowest word error rate among self-supervised speech models under 90M parameters (LibriSpeech, SUPERB)

51.8M parameters, pretrained on about 83,000 hours of English speech on eight NVIDIA B200 GPUs. Matches HuBERT Base on emotion recognition with 55% of its parameters.

Equal contribution. With NYU, Carnegie Mellon, Columbia, Northeastern and Stanford.

Reliable agentic AI

TASR tells a retrieval-augmented LLM agent when to stop searching, using one training-free rule instead of a trained controller that must be rebuilt for every model and task.

+3.42 F1 over fixed three-round retrieval at matched cost, with one threshold across 32 model, retriever and corpus setups

DAWZY maps text, speech and humming to reversible edits in a music workstation through Model Context Protocol tools: 44% task success vs 0% for a comparable baseline, 4.48 of 5 for enjoyment in a 21-person user study.

Lead and corresponding author on TASR.

Digital health and wearable sensing

Lung cancer screening eligibility rests on self-reported smoking history. As technical lead I built an iOS research platform (ResearchKit, HealthKit, SensorKit) that combines Apple Watch sensing, surveys and health records to detect smoking behavior objectively.

~150 adults planned for a 12-month Apple Watch pilot with Sharp HealthCare; software complete, pilot starting

Also: regression, generalized additive and mixed-effects models with geospatial workflows to find environmental risk factors for lung cancer beyond smoking.

U54 CREATE Partnership (SDSU and UC San Diego, NIH/NCI) with Sharp HealthCare.
